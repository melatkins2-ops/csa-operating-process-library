ONE CSA STANDARD

From work to approved practice

An SOP is not finished because a document exists. It is finished when the work is accurate, usable, approved, easy to find, and maintained.

1IdentifyName the recurring work and owner.
2PrioritizeDecide whether the work needs an SOP and how urgent it is.
3DraftBuild from actual practice, requirements, tools, and handoffs.
4ValidatePeople who do or receive the work test it against reality.
5ApproveOwner confirms accuracy and required partners review their parts.
6PublishMake one current approved version easy to find.
7Use & ImproveRevise when practice, requirements, or usability change.

WHEN AN SOP IS THE RIGHT TOOL

Use an SOP when staff need operational consistency.

  • The work is recurring, high-risk, high-impact, compliance-sensitive, or has important handoffs.
  • Roles, decision points, timelines, documentation, or completion expectations need to be explicit.
  • Different people should be able to carry out the work without relying on one person's memory.

DO NOT FORCE IT

Another format may be better.

  • Written process: when people mainly need the flow from beginning to end.
  • Procedure: focused step-by-step instructions for one task.
  • Job aid/tool: checklist, matrix, template, or decision support.
  • Guide: several related resources that belong together.
